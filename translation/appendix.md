# Appendix: pages found only in the Waseda copy

The Waseda University Library copy (文庫11 a0380) is a later issue of the book
from a different publisher, Yamadaya Shōjirō (山田屋庄次郎), rebound as two
volumes. Its story pages are the same as the NDL copy's (one leaf the NDL copy
lacks is translated in its place in Part 3), but it adds an endpaper and a
colophon of its own.

### Endpaper: the Six Jewel Rivers — waseda_vol1_p0002  (scan: 童絵解万国噺 (1861) - Waseda 文庫11 a0380/vol1/p0002.jpg)

Six fan-shaped panels, each carrying one of the classical poems on the "Six
Jewel Rivers" (六玉川, *Mu-Tamagawa*): six rivers named Tamagawa in different
provinces, a favourite subject of poets and print designers. The printed
versions differ in places from the standard texts of the poems.

**Yamashiro: the Jewel River of Ide**
> I'll halt my horse and water him once more —
> where dew from the yellow kerria blossoms
> drips into the Jewel River of Ide.

**Kii: the Jewel River of Kōya**
> Travellers, forgetting, may they not scoop and drink it —
> the water of the Jewel River
> deep in the mountains of Kōya?

[T.N. The poem is a warning: by tradition this stream was poisoned by insects, and the poem tells travellers not to drink.]

**Settsu: the Jewel River**
> Even the wind in the pines sounds lonely enough in autumn —
> and now, in the village by the Jewel River,
> they are beating cloth.

**Musashi: the Jewel River**
> Like the hand-woven cloth they bleach in the Jewel River,
> over and over, why is it
> I long so for someone of long ago?

[T.N. "Over and over" (さら〳〵) puns on the bleaching (さらす) of the cloth; 調布 (*tazukuri*, hand-woven cloth) also names the district.]

**Mutsu: the Jewel River of Noda**
> When evening comes, across the sea wind,
> at the Jewel River of Noda in the far north,
> the plovers are crying.

**Ōmi: the Jewel River of Noji**
> I will come back to seek it — the Jewel River of Noji,
> where bush clover grows, and on its coloured waves
> the moon [comes to] lodge.

[T.N. The end of this poem is worn on the scan ([…] in the transcription); the last line follows the poem's usual sense, the moon reflected on waves tinted by the clover.]

### Colophon — waseda_vol1_p0042  (scan: 童絵解万国噺 (1861) - Waseda 文庫11 a0380/vol1/p0042.jpg)

**Captions:** A picture grid of the ten heavenly stems — Kinoe, Kinoto, Hinoe, Hinoto, Tsuchinoe, Tsuchinoto, Kanoe, Kanoto, Mizunoe, Mizunoto — and the twelve zodiac signs: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Hare, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Sheep, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Boar.

Published by Yamadaya Shōjirō, wholesaler of picture-books and colour prints of the Eastern Capital, Minami-Denmachō 2-chōme.

[T.N. The stems and branches combine into the sixty-year cycle used to name years; the book itself is dated *kanoto-tori* (辛酉), the Metal-Rooster year, 1861.]
