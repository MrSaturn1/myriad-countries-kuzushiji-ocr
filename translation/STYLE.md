# Translation guide — 童絵解万国噺 (Picture Tales of the Myriad Countries, 1861)

Source: the corrected transcriptions in `ocr/final/`. Public-domain 1861 text by
仮名垣魯文 (Kanagaki Robun), pictures by 一孟斎芳虎 (Ichimōsai Yoshitora).

## Voice
- Robun writes for children and women in easy kana, in a lively storyteller's
  gesaku voice: plain, warm, a little theatrical, sometimes punning. Translate
  into clear, natural modern English that keeps that tone — not stiff, not
  modernised slang. A reader should enjoy it as a story.
- Keep his framing devices: "continued from volume two", "the story divides
  here", "continued next time", めでたし〳〵 ("and so all ended happily").
- Don't correct his history. When it departs from fact (Washington slaying a
  mountain monster, Adams fighting a serpent), translate it straight and add a
  short translator's note only where a reader would otherwise be confused.

## Layout of each spread
```
### <Part> <volume/leaf if known> — <job id>  (scan: <folder>/<vol>/<page>.jpg)
**Captions:** "…" / "…"   (picture captions and labels, translated)
<main text, translated, as flowing paragraphs in reading order>
[T.N. …]  translator's notes, sparingly
```
- Follow the `## order` line in each transcription for block order.
- Margins (柱/page numbers): don't translate; cite the leaf in the heading if known.
- Front matter, ads, catalogues, colophons: translate fully but briefly
  (they're of real historical interest: prices, publishers, other books).

## Uncertain text
- 〔字?〕 in the transcription = uncertain reading. Translate the most likely
  sense; if it matters to the meaning, add [?] after the phrase.
- ■ = unreadable. Write […] and, if the gap affects the sense, a T.N.
- Never invent text to fill a gap.

## Names
Use `translation/glossary.md`. Identifiable real people/places get standard
English spellings, with the Japanese form noted in the glossary (not in the
running text). Invented or garbled names: romanise the kana (Meriken, Harina)
and note any likely intended original. Add every new name you meet to your
part's section of the glossary.
