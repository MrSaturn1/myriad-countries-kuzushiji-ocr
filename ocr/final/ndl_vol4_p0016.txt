## notes
The eagle soars out of sight; Adams (あだむす/あゝだむす, 阿丹土) bows in thanks for the immortal's (仙女) divine power, goes down to a valley to drink, and as the sun sets leaves to come back with hired men for the serpent's corpse; he struggles back to the village and that night tells the village headman (むらおさ) the whole story of his mother being swallowed and his revenge with the immortal's help; the headman, marvelling, next morning gathers all the villagers, who go with Adams to the valley, find the serpent (its length could wrap a small hill, its girth like a mortar, its whole body slashed and blood-red) lying dead, are awed by Adams's feat, and set fire to the brushwood they brought (かれしばにひと|もし → 火ともし, completed by p0017's 〔ろ?〕し, which is therefore も). ◬ = solid triangle over hollow triangle, ⊠ = crossed box. だ is printed in a ゞ-like form, hence あだむす (both copies). Unsure: 大〔い?〕に; いち〔ぶ?〕しゞう; 〔ど?〕くじや; めいすに (as printed); 〔ど?〕ふに (perhaps まとふ "wrap around"); に〔ひと?〕しく; the one-glyph columns 〔を?〕 (printed like と) and 〔感?〕 (cursive, read かん by OCR) before せう; margins (柱 and page numbers) cut at the fold — neighbours are numbered 十一/十二 (p0015) and 十三/十四 (p0017), so this spread carries no number of its own or a cut 又-number.
