## notes
Washington (わしんとんげをるげ, of Fairfax in Virginia) apologises to the great man Franklin (ふらんきりん, 拂郎基林) for his rudeness and explains that he had gone to a mountain (かくまくざん) to kill a monster troubling the villages, and on the mountain road came upon what he took for bandits threatening Franklin's younger sister Caroline (加礼留, かれる); she explains she had been carried off by bandits while her brother and father were away. Unsure: the word after the first わしんとん (both OCRs read しめりけん), the worn words after ⊠ (いつなるときか?), the two glyphs before られし, ぎ〔と?〕いへに, 〔ひ?〕ちにようじ, and the end of きりすてん〔とし?〕; Waseda copy is the same spread and confirmed the つゞく box and most of the bottom text. Pillar bears pseudo-Roman letters PATESN plus an unclear glyph.
