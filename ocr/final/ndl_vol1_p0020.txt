## notes
Continuation of an account of a (Russian?) emperor's reforms: he founded schools in the capital, reformed temple rules, invited European scholars versed in military science and artisans, promised the farmers prosperity, opened mines, raised livestock, encouraged farming, built castles, sent scholars to map his territories, set up workshops, subdued the provinces and proclaimed an amnesty (murderers and repentant thieves excepted); by 1717 officials and the people of all provinces offered him an honorific title (尊号). NDL copy is torn at the bottom caption; read mainly from the Waseda copy (same spread). Unsure: すゝ〔む?〕る ending the ▲ block; てんに〔づ?〕る (lite つゞる); をあづ / る (probably for まつりごとをあづかる; no か seen); 〔をそくそ?〕 before そんがう; そうこく vs そうとく; page numbers cut by binding.
