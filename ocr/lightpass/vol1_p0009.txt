## notes
Paris (はりす), France: about 713 streets and 30,000 houses, 600,000 people, theatres and crowds day and night; the ruler is male and succeeds by lineage, laws bind all ranks from high to low officials, and the king makes laws only after agreeing with other kingdoms; the people are honest but rash; canals, trade and shipping thrive; there are schools everywhere, academies where farmers, artisans and merchants study, and workshops in Paris that make weapons, including a big gun called the もるちる (mortar), which is made in a shed ten chō square divided into rooms and stocked with iron, bronze (からかね), copper, etc. Unsure: the glyph after 上/下 (read くわん by both tools, but it may be a cursive 官 + ん); R-bottom cols 2–4 are worn in NDL and read from Waseda (〔ならふ?〕, な〔ほ?〕, ほ〔か?〕百せう = 百姓); なかにも is read as hentaigana な・か(可)・に・も(毛), where both tools had なつにかもん; ま〔ご?〕〳〵 (lite read まごと〳〵); あ〔か?〕がね (lite read ある); the つゞき and 次へ are boxed labels; the 柱 title is cut off at the gutter.
